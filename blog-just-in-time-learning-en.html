The end of "just-in-case" learning: master the skill of just-in-time mastery

In the traditional education system, most people were raised on a "just-in-case" model. They sat through hours of lectures, memorizing formulas and complex syntax, believing that one day they might need that specific information.

But as they step deeper into the age of AI, this model has become obsolete. Information is no longer a scarce resource; it is a flood.

The question is no longer, "How much can you remember?" but rather, "How quickly can you learn exactly what you need to solve the problem in front of you?" This is the essence of just-in-time learning.

The trap of infinite knowledge

Everyone has felt the cognitive load of the modern tech world — the paralyzing feeling that every time you open social media, a new framework or AI model has been released. Trying to learn everything just in case leads to burnout. You spend so much time preparing to build that you never actually build anything.

In a recent livestream, Coyotiv CEO Armağan Amcalar describes the shift in how we process this information:

"Knowledge is everywhere now. In the past, it was difficult to access information; now, we are drowning in it. This is why trying to learn everything in advance is a burden. What makes a difference in the modern world is the ability to pull exactly the information you need at that very moment." — Armağan Amcalar

What is just-in-time learning?

Just-in-time learning is a pedagogical approach where you acquire knowledge at the exact moment it is required to progress. Instead of reading a 500-page book on a language before starting, you start the project and learn the specific concepts needed to complete step one.

Amcalar identifies just-in-time learning as a pillar of modern engineering education. According to him, you learn it as a response to a challenge:

"Don't learn a technology just because it might be useful. Have a problem, have a project. When you encounter an obstacle while doing that project, learn enough to overcome that obstacle and apply it immediately. Real learning happens the moment you get your hands dirty with that solution." — Armağan Amcalar

According to Amcalar, mastering the craft today requires more than just speed; it requires the wisdom to discern exactly what to learn and to what degree. Surface-level understanding has never been sufficient, and in the age of AI, its value has dropped to zero. What remains essential is an insatiable curiosity to dive into the absolute depths of a subject. Paradoxically, it is through just-in-time learning and the current flood of information that an engineer finally gains the time and the resources to achieve this profound depth.

Context over memorization

In a world where AI provides the answers, the human's job is to provide direction. If you don't know the fundamentals, you won't even know what to ask the AI. However, just-in-time learning doesn't mean skipping the basics. It means learning the concepts deeply so you can navigate the tools quickly.

For example, you don't need to memorize every database operator just in case. You need to understand how data works conceptually. This is how senior engineers operate — they don't know everything, but they know how to find and apply anything.

Why Coyotiv embraces the "just-in-time" philosophy

However, at Coyotiv, the curriculum is designed as a single, 6-month journey. You aren't given isolated homework; you are given a project to build. The AI revolution is moving faster than any textbook. If you stay in the just-in-case mindset, you will always be behind. Stop preparing to be a programmer and start being one. Pick a project, find a problem, and learn exactly what you need to solve it — just in time.
